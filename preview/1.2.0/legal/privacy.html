Scope

BlindBox helps you organize photos and videos without creating a BlindBox account. This policy explains on-device processing, the services the app uses, and the controls available to you.

Photos, iCloud and media locations

With your permission, the app uses Apple’s Photos access controls to read authorized photos, videos and related information for previews, organization, favorites, album actions, media details and deletion you confirm. Information may include resource identifiers, filenames, dates, types, dimensions, duration, and camera or location metadata already in the media. You can limit, change or revoke Photos access in system settings.

Media organization primarily happens on your device. If an item is in iCloud, previews, full-quality viewing, metadata requests, playback or export may download it through Apple Photos/iCloud. The data-saving setting reduces some automatic loading; it does not make every feature offline.

When you view details or maps for media containing a location, its coordinates may be sent to Apple’s geocoding or map services to display a place and map. This uses the media’s location, rather than continuously obtaining the device’s live location. Checking for updates may also request app-version information from Apple. See Photos & Privacy and Apple Maps & Privacy.

Confirmed deletion changes the system photo library. With iCloud Photos enabled, deletion may affect synchronized devices; recovery follows Apple’s system rules. Pending-deletion selections and clearing local records do not themselves delete library originals.

Local records, caches and cleanup

The app stores organization progress, pending-deletion selections, viewing history, resource identifiers, statistics, preferences, applicable allowance records, and purchase identifiers and status used to match benefits and avoid repeated feedback. Previews, video restore frames and sharing preparation can also create cached or temporary media files.

Cache controls in Settings affect the corresponding caches only. Files in an active share may be released after sharing ends. Restarting photo organization or resetting watched videos resets the respective records; it does not delete library originals or reset every statistic, allowance or purchase record.

Local records have no single automatic deletion period. Corresponding app functions or the system can clear caches. Revoking Photos access does not erase all local records. Deleting the app through the system normally removes this installation’s local data, but does not automatically delete system backups, shared copies, or records held by Apple or Google. The app does not operate its own cross-device recovery service for your library, viewing history or free allowances; system backups may separately include some app data.

Optional usage analytics

“Settings → Local Stats and Privacy → Share Usage Data” is off by default. The app enables Google’s Firebase Analytics only after you explicitly turn it on. Existing installations without this explicit choice also remain off. You can turn it off in the same place without losing normal photo-library organization. Local statistics and diagnostics are separate from this choice.

When enabled, analytics helps understand feature use and improve the app. The app’s custom events cover launches, pages, organization and deletion actions, counts, onboarding, iCloud loading, share results, updates and rating entry points. Parameters mainly describe feature categories, entry points, counts and error categories. These custom events do not include photo or video files, media filenames, album names, photo resource identifiers or precise photo coordinates.

The SDK may also process an app-installation identifier, device/OS/app versions, language, sessions and usage activity, and an approximate region inferred from the connection’s IP address. It may automatically record qualifying purchase/subscription events, including product, price and currency. Not requiring an account does not make this information fully anonymous. Enabling usage analytics does not also give consent for advertising uses.

Turning it off stops subsequent usage-analytics collection by the app. The app does not save custom events while disabled for later replay. This does not erase previously sent history, requests in transit, existing SDK queues, aggregated reports or external copies. The app currently has no one-tap remote analytics-history deletion feature, and a feedback email address may not match an analytics identifier. See Privacy and Security in Firebase, Analytics data information and data retention information. Aggregated reports and exported copies are not governed by the same retention setting.

Sharing, diagnostics and feedback you choose to send

When you initiate sharing, the app prepares files and hands them to the system share sheet. You decide whether to send them and choose the recipient. Files may retain shooting dates, camera information or location metadata; the app does not uniformly remove all metadata. Recipients and external services handle information under their own rules. Clearing the app’s cache cannot recall copies already sent.

Local diagnostics may include events and times, app/OS versions, device type, action counts, duration, media type and whether location metadata exists. A diagnostic export does not automatically attach library files, photo identifiers or precise coordinates. Log files are cleaned under an approximately 30-day rule based on each file’s last modification time, during writing, reading, summarizing or export. This is not immediate background deletion of each event at expiry. Copies you export are outside this local cleanup rule, and you decide whether to send them.

A feedback-email draft prefills the app version, build, iOS version and device type. You can edit it before sending. Diagnostic files are not attached automatically. The developer receives the email address, message and attachments you actually send to respond to and investigate feedback.

Apple purchases and benefit records

Even without a new purchase, the app may use StoreKit to read app-acquisition evidence, existing purchases, subscriptions and renewal status to check and restore benefits. Information includes app-acquisition/transaction identifiers, products, purchase and expiry dates, and revocation status. The current paid-purchase path checks Apple’s purchase evidence on the device through StoreKit and keeps some benefit and transaction-feedback records locally. It does not separately upload that evidence to a developer-operated paid-receipt server.

When a plan is available and you confirm a purchase, Apple handles payment and your Apple Account. The app does not request your Apple Account password or payment-card number. With optional usage analytics enabled, the SDK may separately record purchase analytics as explained above. See App Store & Privacy.

The current version does not enable the legacy-user gift-verification service under development, and does not send app-acquisition evidence or device-verification material to the developer’s server for that service. This does not disable all Apple or analytics network services. Enabling a new verification service would require updated information about its processing.

Your controls and privacy requests

You can change system Photos permissions, turn off Share Usage Data, use the corresponding cache or organization-history controls, and choose whether to share files or send feedback. Each control has the scope described above. Stopping collection, clearing local data and deleting server history are separate actions.

For privacy questions or requests relating to access, correction or deletion rights, contact mm2m123@icloud.com and describe the feature and request. Do not send your Apple Account password, payment-card number or unnecessary media. An email address alone does not guarantee that every analytics record can be located, or automatically delete Apple purchase records, system backups or recipients’ copies. This policy does not limit rights available under applicable law.
